New World Screwworm Transmission Pathways

GEOG 476 GIS Practicum capstone · client brief from USDA / NGA Tearline · In progress

The problem

Could cattle movement from Guatemala through southern Mexico toward South Texas provide undetected pathways for New World Screwworm, and what explains the June 2026 detections in Zavala County, Texas? Our class is building an intelligence estimate to answer that.

What I did

I am Team Lead for Team A. I keep each member's component connected and set up regular cross-team red-teaming with Team B. My own component is a data reliability check that flags problem detection records using three tests: unit consistency, coordinate plausibility for records with real coordinates, and agreement between U.S. (APHIS) and Mexican (SENASICA) sources. Surprising records go back to their owners instead of being silently "fixed."

Vending Channel

Vending Machine Beverage Availability on the East Quad

GEOG 352 (GNSS in the Geosciences) team project · 2025

The problem

Students and faculty rely on vending machines for quick drinks, but what is in stock varies a lot by location. We wanted to know which machines near the Eller Oceanography and Meteorology Building were most likely to have specific drinks, and how easy they were to reach on foot.

What I did

Our team split up the buildings within about 700 to 800 feet of Eller. Over three days we recorded machine locations as points in Esri Field Maps and logged drink stock in Google Sheets. We then averaged availability across days and mapped the results in ArcGIS Online.

What I found

Langford Architecture Building (A) had the widest variety and highest stock. The Computing Services Annex was the closest option to Eller (not counting in-building machines). The Commons Dining Hall was consistently low. Pepsi was the most common drink and Mountain Dew Baja Blast the least. Some buildings had no machines at all.

INTA Map Channel

INTA Exam Map

Department of Political Science, Texas A&M · 2025

The problem

A graduate-level exam needed a web map that shows and labels only a set list of countries, in a set order, while keeping accurate geometry and working in ArcGIS Online.

What I did

I cleaned the country names in the exam table (Countries_Partial) to match the World Countries Generalized layer, joined the two, and used a definition query to show only matched countries. I then exported the result as a new feature class with an ordering field so the labels keep their sequence.

What I found

Many names did not match: spelling errors, legacy names like "Czechia" vs. "Czech Republic," and politically sensitive cases. I also found that in-memory joins do not carry over when publishing web maps, which is why the export step was needed.
